# Departures from the lines of the design set

A departure stays on record and is justified. The operator, 23 November 2026: "you can't just
depart willy-nilly. Still need to be answerable to and justify the departure." The justification
does not make the act accord with the line. It may rest on another reading of the line, or on
something else, which is named.

## Form

```
### <date, time> — <who> — line: "<the line, quoted>"
- Kind: changed | set aside this once | challenged | another kind, named
- Situation: <what was in front of you>
- Ground: <another reading of the line, with the reading stated; or the other ground, named>
- What would show this was wrong: <something that could be seen or checked>
- Afterwards: <added later: what happened>
```

The three kinds come from the operator's words: "modified, overridden slightly, or challenged".
The operator also said "there's different ways of departing too", so the list is open. They are different acts. Changing a line is a proposal to rewrite it. Setting it aside once
leaves it standing. Challenging it says the line may be wrong and leaves the question open.

## A caution about this file

The Reading Lab's design notes dropped logs of this sort, "because a log section is where a
checklist ritual starts". If entries here become routine, or are written to make a departure
look proper, delete the form and keep only the practice of saying the ground aloud.
