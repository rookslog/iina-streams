# Critiquing a design

**Draft 3.3, 24 November 2026; provisional; not yet marked by the operator; one desk test of the reaction skill (on 3.1, two agents per version) and Codex source checks of 3.1 and 3.2; not used in a project yet.** Part of a set (`../README.md`, which also says what changed). Tags are warrants (`../WARRANTS.md`); `[otherwise: …]` names a source to read beside the line, not its opponent. To change a line, engage its warrant; to depart from one, see the core skill.

Load `designing-with-a-person` first; it binds this skill. **Every line here was new in draft 3.** Its author checked the lines at a desk against two recorded critiques (`docs/design/skill/draft-3/DESK-CHECK-CRITIQUE.md`, made for draft 3, reading 48 of 75 lines of the second); the direction-versus-evidence clause and the clause on exclusions nobody chose came later, in 3.2. No independent trial has been run.

## What you are given

- You are given what the thing is for and who uses it, as kinds of user with their wants. They come from the top of the brief, written under `starting-a-design`. If you were not, say so before anything else [iina-weakened] [chen-omission].
- The brief is given to you as something to judge. It is not the standard [iina-critique-report] [iina-critique].
- If you are given what the person said about the thing, their account of it is evidence and not the answer key; a direction they gave for the work is a direction, not only evidence. If you were not given what they said, say so [op-not-sovereign].

## Finding

- You were launched to find. Report what you find; do not alter the thing [imp-document-not-fix] [patel-review].
- Go by what each kind of user is trying to do, and report whom the design leaves out without its brief saying so. A general instruction to check finds little [iina-problem-form] [diss-check] [implicit-exclusion].
- For each problem say what the user is trying to do, what fails, how you know it (seen in the thing, read in its source, or inferred), and what to change [iina-problem-form].
- Where the build follows the brief and still fails a user, rank it as a problem and say that the brief asked for it [iina-critique-report].
- Keep questions for the brief apart from problems in the build [iina-brief-questions].
- Do not write a missing brief or settle design intent yourself. Say what is missing and hand it back [iina-hand-back] [rl-stays].
- You judge well only what you know. Say which parts of the subject you do not know [diss-skill].

## Judgment and measurement

- Make your own judgment before you read any measurement or detector output. Then set the two side by side [imp-two-assessments] [otherwise: audit-two-contexts].
- Decline a measured finding that conflicts with the platform or with what the thing is for, and say that you declined it [iina-declined].
- Do not lead with a score. If a score is asked for, put it after the problems and say what it does not measure [imp-not-measured] [grunde-ticks] [otherwise: imp-scores].

## Closing

- End with what ran and what did not, what you were and were not given, what you looked for and found lacking, and what weakened the critique [iina-weakened] [imp-untested] [grunde-unfound].
- If another critic worked from the same brief or the same files, say so. Agreement between you is not further evidence [lee-agreement].
- Whether the thing is good for the person stays with the person. Hand that back in plain words [rl-stays].
- For judging how the thing looks, use the project's craft sources alongside this skill. Nothing here replaces them.
