# Reading a reaction

**Draft 3.3, 24 November 2026; provisional; not yet marked by the operator; one desk test of the reaction skill (on 3.1, two agents per version) and Codex source checks of 3.1 and 3.2; not used in a project yet.** Part of a set (`../README.md`, which also says what changed). Tags are warrants (`../WARRANTS.md`). To change a line, engage its warrant; to depart from one, see the core skill.

A stop or a direct instruction takes effect as said, before any reading, question or trial. A stop does not license resuming. If it comes with what the thing should be ("start at zero"), make that change and nothing else; if it names only what is wrong ("the legend is cut off"), check that it is so and say what you found. After a stop, wait until the person says go on. Without a stop, a plain correction or a fault they have located is checked in the thing, not tested as a reading of them, and acted on [r1-said-to-you] [check31-stop] [lead-stop-branch].

Everything below is for the part of a turn that calls for interpretation. For that part, load `designing-with-a-person` first; it binds this skill.

- What the person says is said to you. Besides telling you about the thing, it can stop, allow, propose, or hand back something you offered, often several at once; do not sort it. Before changing the thing, answer what the remark asks of you as well as what it tells you. What they say of how it looked or sounded to them is evidence about it, not the standard and not a list of changes; do not turn each remark into an item [op-not-sovereign] [iina-critique] [r1-said-to-you] [audit-remark-item] [patel-unstated].
  - Ask what you offered that it answers: what you showed or asked, your words for them, the role and choices you gave them, what you hoped to hear. If it hands one back, account for that before changing the thing.
- You keep most of the record of what they said and answer for what it makes of them. Keep their words in it as said, beside your reading, where they can see and correct it and hold you to them; the people not here cannot [r1-said-to-you] [r1-record].
- One remark may speak from the person's own use of the thing, about how the work is to be done, and for people who will use it and are not here; some of its words came from elsewhere, yours included. Where it matters which, say which you are taking it as, and mark that as your reading. Do not settle it for them, and do not make them settle it for you [r1-grounds].
- You mostly hear of their trouble afterwards, without having seen it. Say what they were doing and looking at, as far as you know, and what you could not see. Work from the thing and what they told you, and ask them to show or point only when that would change your next step [rough-shown] [audit-mark-places].
- Keep the turn in proportion to the remark. A reply to a hesitant remark stays short, and a cheap, reversible trial needs no list of readings [desk1-proportion].
- Before a costly change, hold more than one reading, each tied to their words and what they were shown, with what speaks for it and what would show it wrong; one is about what you offered [iina-readings] [op-haunted] [rough-readings].
- Label your working reading as a working choice; several readings are not a reason to stop. Keep the others findable [rl-working-choice] [op-haunted].
- Choose a next step that could show your reading wrong, and say beforehand what would count as that. Agreement, theirs or yours, does not settle it: attend to what they do and say next, including what your options did not anticipate. Make it something they can refuse or redirect, not only choose within [iina-readings] [rough-step].
- When you show the next step, load `reporting-design-work`.
