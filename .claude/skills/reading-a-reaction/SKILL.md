# Reading a reaction

**Draft 3.1, 24 November 2026: draft 3 with redraft 1 and rough pieces 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 applied provisionally by the lead, not yet marked by the operator; not tried with any model.** Lines changed from draft 3 carry a warrant whose entry begins "Provisional". Part of a set; `../README.md` says what the set is. Bracketed tags are warrants, in `../WARRANTS.md`; `[otherwise: …]` marks a source that speaks against the line. To change a line, engage its warrant. To depart from one, see the core skill.

Load `designing-with-a-person` first; it binds this skill.

- What the person says is said to you. Besides telling you about the thing, it can do something: stop the work, allow it, propose, or hand back something you offered, such as a question, a word you used for them, or the way you asked. One remark often does several of these at once; do not sort it. A stop or a direct instruction takes effect as said. What they tell you of how the thing looked or sounded to them is evidence about it, and a fault they have found is evidence to check and act on. For the rest, before you change anything, answer what the remark asks of you as well as what it tells you. It is not a list of changes, and it is not the standard [op-not-sovereign] [iina-critique] [r1-said-to-you].
  - Ask of your reading: what did you offer that this answers (what you showed, what you asked, the words you used for them, the choices you left open)? If it hands one of these back, account for it before you change the thing.
  - Ask: where you write your reading down, are their words kept beside it as they said them, so that they can hold you to them?
- Do not turn each remark into an item [audit-remark-item] [patel-unstated].
- One remark may speak from the person's own use of the thing, about how the work is to be done, and for people who will use it and are not here; some of its words came from elsewhere, yours included. Where it matters which, say which you are taking it as, and mark that as your reading. Do not settle it for them, and do not make them settle it for you [r1-grounds].
- You keep most of the record of what they said: you write it down, cut it and put it into briefs. What that record makes of them is yours to answer for, so keep it where they can see and correct it [r1-record].
- You mostly hear of the person's trouble after it happened and without having seen it. Say what they were doing and looking at when it arose, as far as you know, and what of that you could not see. Where your readings turn on that, ask to be shown [rough-shown] [audit-mark-places].
- Before a costly change, hold more than one reading of the remark, each tied to the person's own words and to what they were shown, each with what speaks for it and against it. One reading is about what you offered: the prompt, the labels, the role you gave the person, what you hoped to hear. Do not run this on a stop, a direct instruction, a plain correction, or a fault the person has already located [iina-readings] [op-haunted] [rough-readings].
- Choose a working reading and label it as a working choice. Several readings are not a reason to stop [rl-working-choice].
- Choose a next step that could show your reading wrong, and say beforehand what would count as that. The person's agreement does not settle it, and neither does yours: attend to what they do and say next, including what your options did not anticipate. Make the step something they can refuse or redirect, not only choose within [iina-readings] [rough-step].
- Keep the readings you did not follow where they can be found [op-haunted].
- If you cannot tell which place a remark is about, ask the person to point at it [audit-mark-places].
- When you show the next step, load `reporting-design-work`.
