# Starting a design

**Draft 3.1, 24 November 2026: draft 3 with redraft 1 and rough pieces 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 applied provisionally by the lead, not yet marked by the operator; not tried with any model.** Lines changed from draft 3 carry a warrant whose entry begins "Provisional". Part of a set; `../README.md` says what the set is. Bracketed tags are warrants, in `../WARRANTS.md`; `[otherwise: …]` marks a source that speaks against the line. To change a line, engage its warrant. To depart from one, see the core skill.

Load `designing-with-a-person` first; it binds this skill.

- Say in one sentence what the thing is for, in the person's words where you have them, before deciding how it looks [audit-purpose] [otherwise: schon-discipline].
- Write who uses it as kinds of user, each with what they want, and set those wants against each other. Do not unify them into one user [op-many-users] [op-tensions] [iina-users] [levinas-third] [otherwise: imp-one-goal].
- For each tension, say how the design responds. A tension may stay open; say that it does [op-tensions].
- Say what every kind of user is owed whatever gets priority, and whom a choice leaves out and what they fall back on [iina-users].
- Ask what is missing from what you were told. What would the person have said by now if it mattered? What have they had no words for? [op-absence] [patel-unstated] [chen-omission]
- Read your own brief for what it got wrong before you build on it. What a brief pins down stops being decided, and what it leaves out is left out of the work [imp-template] [chen-omission].
- A brief written from what the person said is your writing, and the builder will be held to it rather than to them. Keep their words in it as they said them, hedges included, and mark everything else as yours. If you head it with your reading of what they want, date it and sign it as yours: it answers to their words and does not turn them into examples. For each thing the builder must do, say what holds it there: the person asked for it; or you propose it as a try; or a principle the project answers to requires it; or it is your judgment, or the person's, of what people who are not here will need. Do not let one pass for another. Say what is decided, what is still open, what the builder may leave out or decide for itself, and what the check written from the brief will not settle. When something the person says later changes what an earlier remark can still authorise, change the brief and what the review will accept, not only the notes [r1-brief-holds].
- A check that is handed the brief will mostly check against it [iina-critique] [otherwise: iina-critique-report].
- Make one small concrete thing early and show it. Say how rough it is and what it is not meant to show. Do not agree a long description first [grunde-goals] [schon-discipline] [audit-round1] [chen-plan] [otherwise: iina-rough-mock].

## Where this goes, and what it leaves to others

- Put the purpose sentence, the kinds of user and the tensions at the top of the brief. A critic launched later is given exactly these (`critiquing-a-design`).
- For how the thing should look, use the project's craft sources. Nothing here replaces them.
- When you show the small concrete thing, load `reporting-design-work`; when the person answers it, `reading-a-reaction`.
