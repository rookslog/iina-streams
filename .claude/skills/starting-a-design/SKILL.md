# Starting a design

**Draft 3.3, 24 November 2026; provisional; not yet marked by the operator; one desk test of the reaction skill (on 3.1, two agents per version) and Codex source checks of 3.1 and 3.2; not used in a project yet.** Part of a set (`../README.md`, which also says what changed). Tags are warrants (`../WARRANTS.md`); `[otherwise: …]` names a source to read beside the line, not its opponent. To change a line, engage its warrant; to depart from one, see the core skill.

Load `designing-with-a-person` first; it binds this skill.

- Say in one sentence what the thing is for, in the person's words where you have them, before deciding how it looks [audit-purpose] [otherwise: schon-discipline].
- Write who uses it as kinds of user, each with what they want, and set those wants against each other. Do not unify them into one user [op-many-users] [op-tensions] [iina-users] [levinas-third] [otherwise: imp-one-goal].
- For each tension, say how the design responds. A tension may stay open; say that it does [op-tensions].
- Say what every kind of user is owed whatever gets priority, and whom a choice leaves out and what they fall back on [iina-users].
- Look also for whom the design leaves out without anyone having chosen it: through its defaults, through kinds of user the brief did not name, through what its checks test and do not test, and through what you assumed about who uses it and how. Name them, so that leaving them out becomes something someone decides and answers for. Keep this in proportion to the step: for a small, reversible one, a sentence naming whom it may miss is enough [implicit-exclusion].
- Ask what is missing from what you were told. What would the person have said by now if it mattered? What have they had no words for? [op-absence] [patel-unstated] [chen-omission]
- Read your own brief for what it got wrong before you build on it. What a brief pins down stops being decided, and what it leaves out is left out of the work [imp-template] [chen-omission].
- A brief written from what the person said is your writing, and the builder will be held to it rather than to them. Keep their words in it as they said them, hedges included, and mark everything else as yours. If you head it with your reading of what they want, date it and sign it as yours: it answers to their words and does not turn them into examples. For each thing the builder must do, say what holds it there: the person asked for it; or a principle the project answers to requires it; or it is your judgment, or the person's, of what people who are not here will need. Do not let one pass for another. A try you propose is not a requirement: if trying it is within the agreed job, say what is to be tried and that adopting it is still undecided. Say what is decided, what is still open, what the builder may leave out or decide for itself, and what the check written from the brief will not settle. When something the person says later changes what an earlier remark can still authorise, change the brief and what the review will accept, not only the notes [r1-brief-holds].
- A check that is handed the brief will mostly check against it [iina-critique] [otherwise: iina-critique-report].
- Make one small concrete thing early and show it. Say how rough it is and what it is not meant to show. Do not agree a long description first [grunde-goals] [schon-discipline] [audit-round1] [chen-plan] [otherwise: iina-rough-mock].

## Where this goes, and what it leaves to others

- Put the purpose sentence, the kinds of user and the tensions at the top of the brief. A critic launched later is given exactly these (`critiquing-a-design`).
- For how the thing should look, use the project's craft sources. Nothing here replaces them.
- When you show the small concrete thing, load `reporting-design-work`. When the person answers it, a stop or a direct instruction takes effect as said, with nothing to load first; for anything else, load `reading-a-reaction` before changing the work in answer.
