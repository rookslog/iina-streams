## 2. What the lead found (2026-11-27, from 18:20; commit times d92ef17 18:31 onward)

### Crashes (observed in IINA's crash reports)
- **Sixteen IINA crash reports since 22 Nov** in `~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports/IINA-*.ips` (system `ls`). All IINA 1.5.1.
- **The last five share one signature, and it is new.** 26 Nov 00:54, 03:03, 18:24 and 21:42, and 27 Nov 18:16 (the quit the operator reported). Each is on the main thread, inside JavaScriptCore's memory cleanup: a JavaScript string being freed (`WTF::StringImpl::~StringImpl`, during `JSC::Heap` sweep) hits a deliberate assertion (`WTFCrashWithInfo`, line 462 from the crashed thread's first register). Time from IINA launch to crash: about 2.8 h, 11 min, 5.8 h, 24 min and 9 h. Parsed by `scratchpad/crash/parse.py` and `threads.py` (session scratchpad; the reports themselves stay in place).
- **What started each collection:**
  - Four of the five started from a timer, with no Streams or IINA frame on the stack.
  - **Today's (27 Nov 18:16) is different.** Collection started from an allocation inside IINA's plugin bridge, while it was delivering a message from the Open Stream window to Streams (`JavascriptAPIStandaloneWindow.userContentController` → `JavascriptMessageHub.callListener`). This is the first lead on where the damage meets Streams. The lead found it on a second pass over the full 48-frame stack, after a first pass that stopped at 14 frames.
  - In all five, no other thread was running JavaScript, by an approximate filter over thread stacks.
- **Only Streams Dev is switched on today** (`defaults read com.colliderli.iina`: `PluginEnabled.local.iina.streams.dev = 1`, Beta and stable 0). The setting at each crash time isn't recorded, but Dev's trace files were written throughout that period. Dev is running the build of `next` at `9d82dcb`, staged 25 Nov 07:57. IINA's own OpenSubtitles, user-script and yt-dlp plugins were also on.
- **The earlier crashes are other kinds:**
  - 22 Nov: IINA's plugin bridge trapping on a lost page listener (#60) or during Reload All Plugins (#15, #61);
  - 24 Nov: a teardown trap and a segfault.
- **Related (checked):** the two 22 Nov 04:42 reports die at `Heap::requestCollection`, line 2407. That is the same class; see the assertion entry below.
- **Cause: unchecked.** The lead's conjecture is that a JavaScript string is touched outside the JavaScript engine that made it, inside IINA's plugin bridge. A plugin's own JavaScript can't corrupt memory by itself, but how a plugin uses IINA's bridge can provoke a bridge bug. Not ruled out:
  - something added on 24–25 Nov (search requests, follows paging);
  - Dev being a diagnostic build. It is staged with `qa: true`, so it writes trace files on every event and sends a probe in its first minute.
- **The assertion, read in WebKit's source for the exact build** (JavaScriptCore 22625.2.8.11.9, tag `WebKit-7625.2.8.11.9`; the same build in all three reports checked):
  - Line 462 is `AtomStringImpl.cpp:462`: "The string being removed is an atom in the string table of an other thread!"
  - The 22 Nov line 2407 is `Heap.cpp:2407`: the engine's string table isn't the thread's current one.
  - So both kinds are one class: strings crossing between JavaScript engines or threads. Details: #90 comment, 27 Nov.
- **IINA's bridge, from source (helper's read of IINA 1.5.1, three citations spot-checked by the lead).** IINA resolves `iina.http` promises off the main thread (`JavascriptAPIHttp.swift:110`), and runs `mpv.addHook` callbacks off it too (`MPVController.swift:1112-1125`). Streams uses both: 9 `http` call sites, including the 24–25 Nov search and follows requests, and an `on_load` hook. Whether this causes the damage is unchecked. Experiments and the evidence bar: #90 comment of 27 Nov.
- **Correlation tried:** Streams' per-window traces were matched against the crash times. One crash, 26 Nov 18:24, followed a sign-in check by 38 s. The others had no trace within 5 min, because traces are overwritten per channel. Inconclusive.

### Sign-in and follows
- Dev asks Twitch for the follows permission at sign-in (`src/auth.js:2`: `chat:read user:read:follows`).
- Dev's saved Twitch sign-in changed at 18:15 local today (Keychain item attributes only, `mdat`), 90 s before the crash. A fresh sign-in or a token refresh would both do that; which one it was is unknown.
- Dev's log shows the sign-in state going to "off" 1 ms after a player window opened (18:17:16). Read from code (`global.js:355-368`): this is the designed idle stop. When Open Stream closes before the new window has connected, global releases the sign-in, and the window restores it from the Keychain. It is not evidence of being signed out. Not checked in IINA.
- **Why follows don't show while signed in: unchecked.** Candidates:
  - the follows list arrives after typing starts and is held back (W-22);
  - the follows request fails inside IINA, since follows have never been observed working natively;
  - the saved grant still lacks the permission (W-24).

  Dev records nothing about loading follows (no trace), so its log can't decide between them. Whichever it is, the app not showing why is a defect.
- **Each Streams copy keeps its own Twitch sign-in.** The Keychain holds separate items for stable, Beta, Dev and an old candidate copy (attribute names only), so signing in on one copy doesn't sign in the others.

## 5. How we got here: the lead's own account (not independent)

1. **Nobody watched for crashes.** The 22 Nov crashes produced #60 and a 3-hour viewing test (W-1), and that test is still open. The five crashes since 26 Nov went unnoticed for two days because nothing tells the lead when IINA crashes.
2. **Effort went to narrow slices, reviewed heavily, while nobody walked the whole app.**
   - Of 183 commits since 23 Nov, 58 name #74 (Open Stream suggestions and search).
   - That one feature took three revisions and a 1000-sequence property test for which row is selected.
   - The Dev update tool took 12 commits and three cross-model review rounds.
   - Meanwhile no one used the app the way `PRODUCT.md` §Users describes, from each start point, natively.
3. **The roadmap put the basics late.**
   - Sign-in and account consistency (#6) sits at 0.8.0.
   - The plugin panel's design review (#20) and controls on IINA's playback bar (#52) sit at 0.6.0.
   - 0.5.6 went to watch history and search (D-035).
   - That order is the lead's proposal, approved in D-035, made before the app's whole journey had been tried.
4. **Decisions that were the lead's went to the operator, in vocabulary they hadn't seen.** There are 20 open waiting items. W-26 is the clearest case: it asked them to accept or refuse risks to the lead's own developer tooling, risks whose worst case is a rebuild. The review rule (D-032) says a class that survives review goes to the operator. The lead applied it to a question that wasn't theirs.
5. **"Proof is native" (PRODUCT.md principle 5) was not held to.**
   - Search was merged as provisional with follows never seen working in IINA.
   - The operator's daily Streams copy is a diagnostic build. That variable was never separated from the crashes.
6. **Sign-in is split by copy.** Dev, Beta and stable each keep their own Twitch sign-in, which works against "signed in across the board". The split exists so test copies can't damage the stable sign-in. Whether a shared sign-in is safe is unexamined.

## 6. Order (proposed; **approved by the operator 2026-11-27 18:41, D-036**: "go ahead, pause features and fix the basics first")

1. **Pause feature work.** Nothing new merges into `next` except fixes. The rest of 0.5.6 waits.
2. **Crashes first.**
   - (a) The lead checks for new IINA crash reports at the start of every session and after every native test, and records each one.
   - (b) Investigate the JavaScript-cleanup crash: switch the operator's Dev copy to a normal (non-diagnostic) build; read IINA's plugin bridge for where strings cross between JavaScript engines; try to reproduce under load in a separate IINA session; report upstream if it is IINA's.
   - Done means a cause with evidence, or a mitigation that holds through the operator's ordinary viewing.
3. **Walk every journey natively.**
   - The lead walks each start point in IINA, signed out and signed in, recording each with screenshots against `PRODUCT.md` §Users: cold open, ⌃⌘O, a pasted link, a link sent from the browser, an offline channel, a VOD, live, switching.
   - An independent reviewer from another model family walks the same list.
   - Output: defects by journey, including the collapsing menu and the playback problems.
4. **Re-plan around the journeys.**
   - Sign-in and account consistency (#6), the plugin panel's design (#20) and IINA's playback bar (#52) move ahead of the rest of 0.5.6.
   - Brought to the operator as one decision, with its cascade.
5. **Process changes (the lead's proposal, in effect unless the operator refuses):**
   - decisions about the lead's own tooling are the lead's;
   - a waiting item is written only when only the operator can settle it, in plain words;
   - internal tooling gets one review round, not three.

## 7. Independent account

Written by Codex (gpt-6.2-sol, xhigh, read-only), a different model family from the lead's, on 27 Nov. Its first launch, at 18:31, sat waiting on keyboard input and produced nothing, because the lead launched it without closing its input. It was relaunched at 19:09 and finished by 19:14. The brief had it form its own account before reading §5. Full text: [2026-11-27-independent-account.md](2026-11-27-independent-account.md).

Where it differs from the lead's §5, in short:
- **Crashes were warned of earlier.** They were reported on 7 Nov, and on 22 Nov three viewing sessions crashed. Stability should have displaced features then.
- **The lead understated its own role in the defects.** Its search spec contradicted the approved mock, the tests enforced the wrong design, and the operator had already called out attention to detail on 23–24 Nov.
- **Native acceptance was made subordinate to implementation.** Features were merged without IINA proof, against the project's own evidence hierarchy.
- **D-032 was over-applied.** Of the escalations, only product scope, privacy, public releases, upstream posts and access to the operator's IINA were theirs.
- **Separate sign-ins per copy are intentional isolation,** not the cause of inconsistency within one copy. The lead accepts this correction to §2 and §5 item 6.
- **A separate test environment should come now,** not before launch.

Its order matches D-036: reliable playback, then the whole Twitch experience, then the re-plan, plus independent native testing now. It adds that success is measured by ordinary use becoming dependable, not by finished reviews.
